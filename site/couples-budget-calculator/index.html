    How the income-based split works

    With an income-based split, each person covers the same percentage of their own income. The share is the shared expenses multiplied by that person's income divided by the couple's total income.

    Example: if shared expenses are $1,500 and the incomes are $2,000 and $3,000, total income is $5,000. Partner A covers 40% ($2,000 / $5,000) and pays $600, Partner B covers 60% and pays $900. The weight on income is identical: 30% in both cases.

    50/50 or income-based?

    A 50/50 split is the simplest: half each, no math. It works well when incomes are similar. When incomes are very different, though, the same amount weighs much more on the partner who earns less.

    An income-based split is fairer in that case, but it needs a calculation and should be updated when incomes change. In short: similar incomes use 50/50; different incomes use income-based.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do you split shared expenses as a couple?

    With a 50/50 split each person covers half of the shared expenses. With an income-based split, each person covers the same percentage of their own income: multiply the shared expenses by that person's income divided by the couple's total income.

    What is the difference between a 50/50 split and an income-based split?

    A 50/50 split divides expenses equally, so it weighs more on the partner who earns less. An income-based split makes expenses weigh the same on both incomes, so the higher earner pays more.

    Does the calculator save or send my data?
